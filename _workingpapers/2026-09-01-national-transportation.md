blurb: 'How do credit banking and durable vehicle investment change the timing and cost of shifting decarbonization effort among road fuels, electrification, and aviation fuels? A dynamic national model spanning light-, medium-, and heavy-duty vehicles and aviation compares policy packages with a carbon-price path that achieves the same cumulative reductions.'
excerpt: 'Clean-fuel credits can be banked, vehicle fleets and charging networks accumulate over time, and today''s decisions depend on expectations about future prices and policy, so evaluations that treat each year in isolation can misstate both cost and emission reductions. This paper builds a dynamic national model spanning light-, medium-, and heavy-duty vehicles and aviation, with credit banking across years. It compares current policy and reform scenarios with a carbon-price path that achieves the same cumulative emission reductions, and traces differences in cost and welfare across drivers, industries, and taxpayers.'
