blurb: 'How do expectations, credit banking, and investment shape the cost of overlapping federal and state climate policy in U.S. transportation? I estimate how credit holders revise their expectations and how biofuel capacity responds to margins, and compare current policy with a carbon price that achieves the same cumulative abatement.'
excerpt: 'Clean-fuel credits can be banked, plants and vehicle fleets accumulate, and today''s decisions depend on what firms expect future prices and rules to be. California''s credit bank quadrupled after 2022 while the price stayed positive, a pattern no fixed foresight horizon reproduces. Using CARB and EPA market data, I estimate how credit holders update their expectations and how renewable diesel capacity responds to expected margins, and embed these estimates in a dynamic national model of fuel, vehicle, and aviation credit markets. The model compares current policy and reform scenarios with an equal-abatement carbon price and traces the costs to drivers, farmers, and taxpayers.'
