excerpt: 'China''s central environmental inspections send central teams into a province, open a complaint hotline, and hand cases to local governments to investigate and resolve. Using case-level records for more than 80,000 complaints across two inspection rounds, preliminary results indicate an overall decline in escalated enforcement between rounds, with a larger decline for substantiated complaints against small service businesses than for those against heavy industry. The divergence appears at the disposition stage, where local officials have the greatest discretion. The paper asks whether it reflects learning about central priorities, changes in what citizens report, or limits on indiscriminate shutdowns, and traces environmental consequences through plant emissions and satellite air-quality data.'
