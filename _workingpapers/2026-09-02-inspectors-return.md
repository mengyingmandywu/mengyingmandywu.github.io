excerpt: 'China''s central environmental inspections send central teams into a province, open a complaint hotline, and hand cases to local governments to investigate and resolve. Case records for more than 80,000 citizen complaints across two inspection rounds show that verified complaints against small service businesses became less likely to be escalated to penalties than those against heavy industry. The change comes at the sanction stage, where local officials have the most discretion. The paper asks whether it reflects learning about central priorities, changes in what citizens report, or limits on indiscriminate shutdowns, and traces environmental consequences through plant emissions and satellite air-quality data.'
