What a policy says and what it does are different things. I ask three questions about the difference: what the policy costs, who pays, and whether the change lasts. I study these questions in multi-level systems, using equilibrium models that carry the engineering constraints and crediting rules firms face, administrative data analyzed with causal designs and text-as-data methods, and fieldwork with regulators and firms in the United States and China.
