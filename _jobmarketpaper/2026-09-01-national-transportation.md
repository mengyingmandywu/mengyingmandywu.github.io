blurb: 'Federal and state climate policies stack on the same gallon of U.S. transportation fuel. The stack abates at about $180 a tonne, and extending the federal production credit while the federal mandate binds mostly lowers fuel prices and raises emissions.'
excerpt: 'The United States cuts emissions from transportation fuel by stacking a federal blending mandate, a federal production credit and clean fuel standards in four states. I ask what this stack costs and what one more federal dollar buys on top of it. I build a dynamic model of U.S. fuel and vehicle markets for 2026–2045. I estimate investment from 46 capacity decisions and credit holders'' expectations from state credit prices and banks, and I test both on history. Current law abates at about $180 a tonne and captures less than a tenth of the welfare gain of a carbon price. Because the federal mandate binds, extending the production credit beyond 2029 sends about three quarters of each federal dollar to fuel buyers and raises emissions. Whether it abates anything depends on EPA''s mandate, not on the credit. California''s credit prices imply a bank lasting decades longer than fundamentals do, and expectations change when its standard reaches the cap, not what the stack costs.'
