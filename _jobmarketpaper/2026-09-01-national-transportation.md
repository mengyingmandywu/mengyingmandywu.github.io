blurb: 'How do expectations shape the cost of overlapping federal and state climate policy? I estimate how credit holders, biofuel investors, and electric-vehicle buyers respond to policy, and show how expectations change what an additional federal dollar buys.'
excerpt: 'Federal and state governments subsidize the same low-carbon fuels through overlapping credit markets. Credit prices reflect what participants expect these policies to become, not only current rules. I estimate how credit holders form expectations, how biofuel producers invest, and how households adopt electric vehicles, and build a dynamic equilibrium model of U.S. fuel, vehicle, and aviation markets with each state clean-fuel program modeled separately. The paper shows how expectations determine whether an additional federal dollar buys new abatement, transfers to fuel users, or relocation of fuel across states.'
