excerpt: 'Three layers of climate policy apply to the same gallon of U.S. transportation fuel: a federal blending mandate, a federal production tax credit, and state clean fuel standards with banked credits. I ask what this stack buys and what one more federal dollar adds to it, including when the effects arrive. I build a dynamic model of the federal and state credit markets, with investment and credit-price expectations estimated from market data. Current law abates carbon at about $134 a tonne and captures about 15% of the welfare gain of a carbon price. Because the federal mandate binds, a biofuel subsidy lowers the price of the mandate’s credits instead of adding biofuel. Extending the production credit would send about three quarters of each federal dollar to fuel buyers, and emissions would rise by 64 million tonnes. A carbon price would achieve the same abatement as a higher mandate at about $32 a tonne instead of $163. State credit prices sit far below what the statutes imply, as if holders expected scarcity decades from now. Low prices generate fewer credits, so this doubt makes California’s standard reach its price cap sooner; the cost of the stack hardly changes.'
