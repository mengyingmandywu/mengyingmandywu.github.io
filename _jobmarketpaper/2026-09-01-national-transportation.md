excerpt: 'Three climate policies cover U.S. transportation fuel: a federal biofuel mandate, a federal clean-fuel tax credit, and clean fuel standards in four states. I ask what this stack buys and at what cost, using a dynamic equilibrium model of fuel, feedstock and credit markets for 2026–45 in which credit holders’ expectations and biofuel investment are estimated and tested on years outside the estimation sample. The federal mandate binds: it sets how much biofuel the country uses, so the tax credit and the state standards change emissions only through fuel prices, vehicle choice and where fuel is burned. About half of the emissions cut inside the four states is renewable diesel drawn there from other states; the rest comes from higher fuel prices and electric vehicles. Where fuel also falls under cap-and-trade, the standards either cost the state allowance revenue or cut nothing, and either way cost more than they are worth. If EPA keeps the mandate's percentages at their 2027 level, extending the tax credit past 2029 adds almost no biofuel. It lowers fuel prices and raises emissions by 33 to 67 million tonnes. Current law abates 1.8 billion tonnes of CO2 at about $149 a tonne; the next 100 million tonnes would cost $36 a tonne under a carbon price and $175 under a larger mandate.'
