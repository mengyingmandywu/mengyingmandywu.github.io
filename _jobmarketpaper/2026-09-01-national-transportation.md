excerpt: 'Congress must decide whether to extend 45Z, the federal production tax credit for clean transportation fuel, beyond 2029. The credit is paid on gallons that are already regulated: the federal Renewable Fuel Standard requires refiners to blend biofuel, and California, Oregon, Washington and New Mexico run clean fuel standards with bankable credits and capped prices. I ask what an extension would achieve on top of these policies. I build a dynamic model of U.S. fuel and vehicle markets in which the federal and state credit markets clear each year, and estimate investment, electric-vehicle and charger adoption, and credit holders’ expectations from market data. As long as the federal mandate binds, it fixes how much biofuel is used, so an extension mainly lowers the price of RINs, the credits refiners buy to comply. If EPA keeps the mandate unchanged, about three quarters of each federal dollar reaches fuel buyers as cheaper fuel and emissions rise by 64 million tonnes over 2030–45; emissions fall only if EPA raises the mandate at the same time. Current law as a whole abates carbon dioxide at about $134 a tonne and achieves about 15% of the welfare gain from a carbon price set at the social cost of carbon. State credit prices have stayed far below what the statutes imply. Under the expectation rule I estimate from these prices, low prices mean that fewer credits are generated, so California’s bank runs out and its credit price reaches the cap earlier and more abruptly than under perfect foresight.'
