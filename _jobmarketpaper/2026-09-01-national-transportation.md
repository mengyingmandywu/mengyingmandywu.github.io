excerpt: 'In the United States, a federal blending mandate, a federal production tax credit and state clean fuel standards with banked credits all apply to the same gallon of transportation fuel. I ask what additional federal support for clean fuel achieves in this setting and when its effects arrive. The answer depends on which regulatory constraint binds. I build a dynamic model of the federal and state credit markets in which investment and an expectation rule for credit prices are estimated from market data. Current law abates carbon at about $134 a tonne and achieves about 15% of the welfare gain of a carbon price. Because the federal mandate binds, a permanent extension of the federal production credit would, at fixed mandate percentages, mainly lower the price of the mandate’s credits: about three quarters of each federal dollar would reach fuel buyers, and emissions would rise by 64 million tonnes. State credit prices are well below the levels the statutes imply. Under the estimated expectation rule, California’s standard reaches its price cap earlier and more abruptly than under perfect foresight.'
