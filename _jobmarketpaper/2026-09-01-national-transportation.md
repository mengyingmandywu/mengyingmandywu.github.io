excerpt: 'In U.S. transportation fuel, a federal mandate with tradable credits, a federal tax credit, and four state clean fuel standards with credit banks and price caps all apply to the same gallon. This paper asks what a federal climate dollar buys in that stack. The answer depends on which limit binds and on what the market expects, and the two have come apart. Since California''s 2024 amendments took effect, its credit prices have stayed near a quarter of the cap, although under the rules its bank runs out in the early 2030s. Prices of federal mandate credits put the chance that a lapsed tax credit would be restored at 0.18, yet every lapse has been restored. I build a model of U.S. fuel and vehicle markets for 2026 to 2045 that solves the federal market and all four state markets together, with investment estimated from 46 capacity decisions and tested out of sample. Under either expectation rule, California hits its price cap in 2032 or 2033. Because the federal mandate already binds, about 73 cents of each dollar of an extended federal credit goes to cheaper fuel, almost none reaches state markets, and emissions do not fall further.'
