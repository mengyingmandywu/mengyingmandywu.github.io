I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Salata Institute for Climate and Sustainability and the Belfer Center at Harvard Kennedy School, and I received my Ph.D. from MIT. I study how climate policy performs once it passes through the markets and bureaucracies that deliver it, when more than one level of government sets the terms. In the United States, federal and state programs credit the same fuels under different rules; in China, central targets reach firms through local officials who decide which complaints become sanctions. In both settings, how one layer of policy performs depends on what firms and officials expect the other layers to do.

My job market paper, {{ jmp.title }}, asks what a federal climate dollar buys when a federal mandate, a federal tax credit, and four state clean fuel standards all apply to the same gallon of fuel.
