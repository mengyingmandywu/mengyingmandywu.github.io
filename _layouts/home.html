I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Salata Institute for Climate and Sustainability and the Belfer Center at Harvard Kennedy School. I received my Ph.D. from MIT's Institute for Data, Systems, and Society.

I study the cost and incidence of climate policy when several levels of government regulate the same market. In U.S. transportation fuel, federal and state programs overlap; I build dynamic equilibrium models that estimate how credit holders and investors form expectations, and use them to measure what an additional policy buys and who pays. In China, I study delegated enforcement: the central government inspects provinces, local officials decide which violations are sanctioned, and I use administrative records and causal designs to test whether the gains last.

My job market paper, {{ jmp.title }}, asks what a federal climate dollar buys when a federal mandate, a federal tax credit, and four state clean fuel standards all apply to the same gallon of fuel.
