I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Salata Institute for Climate and Sustainability and the Belfer Center at Harvard Kennedy School. I received my Ph.D. from MIT's Institute for Data, Systems, and Society.

I study the cost and incidence of climate policy when several levels of government regulate the same market. In U.S. transportation fuel, I build dynamic equilibrium models of overlapping federal and state credit markets, with expectations and investment estimated from market data. In China, I study delegated enforcement, using administrative records and causal designs to test whether the gains from central inspections last.

My job market paper, {{ jmp.title }}, finds that the federal and state policies stacked on U.S. transportation fuel abate carbon at about $134 a tonne. Because the federal mandate binds, extending the federal production credit would mostly lower fuel prices and raise emissions.
