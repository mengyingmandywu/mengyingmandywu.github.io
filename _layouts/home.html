I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Salata Institute for Climate and Sustainability and the Belfer Center at Harvard Kennedy School. I received my Ph.D. from MIT's Institute for Data, Systems, and Society.

I am an energy and environmental policy scholar. Transportation is the largest source of U.S. greenhouse gas emissions, and it is governed by overlapping federal and state policies. I study what these policies buy, what they cost, and who pays. I designed and built an estimated equilibrium model of U.S. fuel and vehicle markets that covers cars, trucks, and aviation fuel and links them through federal and state credit markets. In China, I study how environmental rules are enforced, using administrative records and causal designs to test whether the gains from central inspections last.

My job market paper, {{ jmp.title }}, asks what the federal and state climate policies stacked on U.S. transportation fuel buy and at what cost. Because the federal biofuel mandate binds, extending the federal clean-fuel tax credit past 2029 adds almost no biofuel; it lowers fuel prices and raises emissions.
