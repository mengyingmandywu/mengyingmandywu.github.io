I am an energy and environmental policy scholar. My research asks how climate policy performs once it passes through the markets and bureaucracies that deliver it, with a focus on multi-level systems, where authority is divided across governments. In the United States, federal and state programs credit the same fuels under different rules; in China, central targets reach firms through local officials who decide which complaints become sanctions. In both settings, how one layer of policy performs depends on what firms and officials expect the other layers to do.

My job market paper, {{ jmp.title }}, asks how expectations shape the cost of overlapping federal and state climate policy, and what an additional federal dollar buys.
