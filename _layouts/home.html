I study how climate and environmental policy performs once it passes through the markets and bureaucracies that deliver it.

I am an energy and environmental policy scholar and a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Salata Institute for Climate and Sustainability, jointly appointed to the Environment and Natural Resources Program at the Harvard Kennedy School Belfer Center. I ask three questions about any policy: what it costs, who pays, and whether the change lasts. Ph.D., MIT.

Policy layering in U.S. transportation
What the rules sayFederal fuel standards, tax credits, and state clean-fuel programs, each written on its own.
One feedstock marketRoad and aviation fuels compete for the same limited biofuel feedstocks.
What happensFeedstock flows to California road fuel. Aviation fuel reaches about 2.5 percent of the 2030 target.
Delegated enforcement in China
What the center setsCentral teams inspect a province and open a complaint hotline.
Local officials decideLocal governments choose which complaints become sanctions.
What reaches the plantSO2 falls while inspectors are on site. More than half the reduction reverses after they leave.

What a policy says and what it does are different things. Both lines of my work measure the difference.
