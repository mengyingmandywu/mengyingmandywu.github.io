I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Salata Institute for Climate and Sustainability and the Belfer Center at Harvard Kennedy School. I received my Ph.D. from MIT's Institute for Data, Systems, and Society.

I am an energy and environmental policy scholar. Climate and environmental rules are rarely set by one government alone, and I study what happens when national and local governments share the job. Transportation is the largest source of U.S. greenhouse gas emissions, and both federal and state rules apply to its fuel. I designed and built an estimated dynamic equilibrium model of fuel, vehicle, and credit markets, from cars and trucks to aviation fuel, to measure what these overlapping rules achieve and who pays. In China, the central government sends inspectors but relies on local officials to enforce. I study whether compliance lasts after the inspectors leave and whom local enforcement reaches.

My job market paper, {{ jmp.title }}, finds that the federal biofuel mandate is the layer that binds, so the tax credit and state standards mostly change who pays. With the mandate held at its 2027 level, extending the tax credit past 2029 would raise emissions by 33 to 67 million tonnes.
